Anne and Sally were spending the afternoon in a small seaside town, the kind where narrow streets wound between pastel houses and the smell of salt lingered in the air. They had just finished buying sweets from a corner shop when Anne suggested they walk to the old lighthouse on the edge of the harbor. Sally agreed, clutching her favorite red marble in her hand as they made their way along the cobblestone path.

When they reached the lighthouse garden, Sally noticed a wooden bench near a patch of flowers. “I want to play a game,” she said, placing her marble inside a small blue box she had been carrying. She set the box carefully under the bench. “I’ll leave it here while we explore.” Anne nodded, watching closely as Sally hid the marble. Just then, a boy named Leo, who lived nearby, wandered into the garden. He saw Sally’s box under the bench after she and Anne had walked toward the lighthouse tower.

Curious, Leo picked up the blue box, opened it, and found the marble inside. He looked around, then decided to move it. Across the garden, near a tall oak tree, there was a metal bucket used for watering plants. Leo placed the marble inside the bucket and left the empty blue box where he had found it. A gardener named Mrs. Ruiz saw Leo near the bucket but didn’t notice what he had done with the marble.

A little while later, Sally and Anne returned from climbing the lighthouse stairs. Sally ran ahead, excited to retrieve her marble. Anne slowed down, noticing the gardener and the bucket under the oak tree. Sally went straight to the bench and opened the blue box. It was empty. She frowned, confused.

Anne paused for a moment. She knew that Leo had moved the marble to the bucket, but Sally had not seen that happen. Sally still believed the marble was in the box under the bench, exactly where she had left it.

Anne turned to Leo, who was now sitting by the garden gate. “Sally thinks her marble is still in the box,” Anne said quietly. Leo looked surprised, realizing that Sally didn’t know what he had done.

Meanwhile, Sally kept searching inside the box and around the bench, growing more puzzled. “Where could it be?” she asked. Anne gently pointed toward the oak tree. “Maybe we should check over there too,” she suggested, thinking about what Sally believed versus what was actually true.

As they walked toward the bucket, Anne wondered whether Sally would understand what had happened once she found the marble—and whether Leo would explain why he had moved it in the first place.
