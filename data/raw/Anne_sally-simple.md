Anne and Sally were playing together in the garden one sunny afternoon. Anne had a small blue box, and Sally had a red basket. They decided to play a game where they would hide a shiny marble and then try to find it again. Sally placed the marble carefully inside her red basket and smiled, pleased with her hiding spot.

After a while, Sally said she needed to go inside the house to get a snack. She left the garden, making sure Anne saw that the marble was still in the basket. While Sally was away, Anne looked at the basket, then at her blue box. She had an idea. Quietly, Anne took the marble out of Sally’s basket and placed it inside her own blue box, closing the lid gently.

A few minutes later, Sally returned to the garden, still thinking about the marble she had hidden earlier. She looked around, remembering exactly where she had left it. Anne watched her closely, curious about what Sally would do next.

Sally walked over to her red basket and opened it, expecting to find the marble inside. Anne, who knew the marble was now in the blue box, wondered whether Sally would look somewhere else or stick with her original hiding place. The question was simple but important: where would Sally look for the marble first?

This story helps us think about how different people can have different beliefs. Even though Anne knows the marble has been moved, Sally does not. Sally’s actions depend on what she believes to be true, not on what is actually true.
